Where our information comes from

Last updated 10 October 2026

Is your name in here? Write to hello@dancersdna.com to ask what we hold, to have it corrected or removed, or to object. You don't need an account or a reason. A person reads every message.

What we keep

Before dancersdna opens, we keep a staff-only record of professional credits on cruise-ship productions — for the creatives who made the shows (directors, choreographers, designers, composers and so on) and the performers in their casts.

For each person we write down:

  • their name;
  • what they did — choreographer, dancer, singer, and so on;
  • the show, the ship, and the company behind it;
  • the public page we read it on, and the date.

All of it comes from public sources: producers' and cruise lines' own pages, press releases and posts. We read them one at a time. We don't buy lists of people.

What we don't keep

No contact details — no email address, phone number or postal address. No date of birth. Nothing private. Where a public post tagged someone by their social-media name, we note that tag as part of where the credit came from.

Nothing is published

None of these names appears anywhere the public can read. Not on a page, not in a search result, not in a list. Showing a name publicly would be a separate decision, and it has not been made.

How many

Around 2,000 people so far. The number grows as we read.

Why we haven't written to each person

When we take someone's name from somewhere other than them, the normal rule is that we tell them. There is an exception where telling each person would take a disproportionate effort, and we rely on it: for most of these people we have no way to reach them, and finding one for each would be a far bigger job than the reading. This page is part of what that exception asks of us in return.

One thing cuts against us, and we'd rather say it: this information is weeks or months old, not decades.

Who we don't keep on this basis. We don't keep the names of people on land productions, or of anyone with their own professional contact route — their own website, their agency, a union listing — until we can tell them ourselves.

Our lawful basis

Legitimate interests: keeping an accurate, sourced history of who made and performed these shows, so the credit goes to the right people. A professional credit that the industry already published is about the least private thing there is about a person, and we keep nothing more than that.

Who is responsible

Elias Schinas, in Cyprus. Write to hello@dancersdna.com.

Your rights

You can ask what we hold about you, have it corrected, have it removed, or object to us keeping it. We answer within one month, and a request to take something out is treated as the kind that cannot wait. If you think we have handled your information badly, you can complain to the Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection in Cyprus — dataprotection.gov.cy.
